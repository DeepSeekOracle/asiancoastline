Lyrics and copyright: what you can share

Lyrics are a written work inside a song. Listening free does not make the words free to republish, sell, or feed to a commercial model. Here is the practical line.

Lyrics are their own copyright

A song usually contains at least two copyrights: the composition (words and melody) and the sound recording. The lyrics page on this site is a reference for listeners, not a public-domain text dump. The words are protected even when the stream is free to hear.

That means you can listen, quote a short line in a review with attribution, and link to the page. You cannot copy the full lyric into another site, print it on merchandise, or include it in a commercial product without permission.

What is usually fine

  • Quoting a line or two in a review, essay, or classroom discussion, with attribution.
  • Linking to the lyric page instead of copying it.
  • Singing along, performing privately, or using the words in a personal notebook.
  • Discussing the meaning, structure, or imagery without reproducing the whole text.

What needs permission

  • Republishing a full lyric on another website or app.
  • Printing lyrics on merchandise, posters, or packaging for sale.
  • Using lyrics in a video, podcast, game, or ad (sync and commercial use).
  • Feeding the lyrics into a commercial model or dataset for resale.

Sync, samples, and AI

A sync license is the permission to pair music or lyrics with moving images. It is separate from listening and separate from mechanical reproduction. If you want to use a track in a video, ask before you publish, even if the video is free.

AI training is its own permission question. A public stream is not a license to train a commercial model on the catalog and sell a derivative. If you are researching or building a personal tool, say so and ask; the answer may still be no, but the request is the honest starting point.

How to request permission

Email excavationstation@gmail.com with the track title, the intended use, the platform, and whether money is involved. A clear request gets a clear answer faster than a vague one. If the use is commercial, include a timeline.

If you are unsure whether your use is personal or commercial, describe it plainly. The terms and the streams vs masters guide explain the listening license; this page is about the words.

How to credit a lyric properly

A useful credit names the song, the recording artist, the songwriter if different, and the source you are quoting from. For a review, a format like “‘line’ from ‘Title’ by Excavationpro, from the asiancoastline.com lyric page” is enough. The link matters: it lets a reader check the context instead of trusting a screenshot.

Common mistakes are easy to avoid. Do not attribute a lyric to the wrong artist because a cover version is more famous. Do not present a paraphrase as an exact quote. Do not strip the songwriter credit when the composition and the recording have different owners. Do not use a lyric as a headline if the line is the whole hook and the article is commercial.

If you are unsure, quote less and link more. A short, accurate quotation with a working link is better than a full reproduction with a vague credit.

What about public-domain lyrics?

Some older songs are in the public domain, but the recording of them may still be protected. A public-domain lyric does not automatically make a specific recording free to use. Check the composition and the recording separately; the streams vs masters guide explains why those are different objects.

For modern catalog music, assume the lyric is protected until you have a written reason to think otherwise. That assumption is boring, and it keeps you out of the most common copyright mistake.

If you need a longer excerpt for a classroom or a private study group, keep it inside that setting and cite the source. Publishing the same excerpt on a public site is a different use with a different answer.

FAQ

Can I quote a lyric in a review?

A short quotation with attribution is usually fine. Reproducing the full lyric is not.

Can I put lyrics on a T-shirt?

Not without permission. Merchandise is a commercial use of the composition.

Can I use a song in my YouTube video?

Ask first. That is a sync use, not the same as listening free on the site.

Can I train an AI on the lyrics?

A public stream is not a training license. Ask before using the catalog in a commercial model.
